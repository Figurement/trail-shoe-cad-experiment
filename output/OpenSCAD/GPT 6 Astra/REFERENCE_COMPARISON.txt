WHAT DID NOT MATCH, AND WHAT CHANGED

Lateral upper
  The earlier broad band did not describe the inset black blade or its lower
  orange edge correctly. The revised image-space sketches include the upper
  branch, blade cutout and lower rim. The lower return and teardrop opening
  follow the photographed contours instead of a generic loop. The black
  support's forward boundary now terminates at the sole rather than leaving
  unsupported pointed extensions inside the open region.

Medial upper
  The former large zigzag did not preserve the visible textile windows.
  Separate forward support contours and the larger rear window replace that
  broad fill. The heel accent follows the photographed tapered approach and
  inward curved notch; it is not a mirror of the lateral frame.

Sole and heel
  The earlier upper edge dropped too early through the midfoot, and the
  channel read as a uniform layered strip. The profile now holds the rear
  height longer, drops toward the forefoot and rises into the toe rocker.
  Rail height and tapered lateral/medial relief are separately controlled.
  The heel cup and channel arch transversely; the rubber/foam boundary has
  the visible central downward notch rather than a flat rear edge.
  The midsole remains continuous. The carrier and upturned rubber wraps are
  a single closed lofted sheet, with no separate color-band foam solids.

Collar, tongue and eyestays
  The collar opening was too deeply V-shaped and the tongue crest sat too
  far rearward. The rim and tongue-section positions have been revised.
  The first two lace positions belong to the large upper eyestay plate in
  the photographs, not two separate small pads. That plate and the three
  lower positions are now represented accordingly.
  The flattened fabric laces and the separate cougar graphic are retained.

Rear wrap and compound-curvature follow-up
  Four separate visible rear accents were traced from the heel photograph:
  the medial and lateral upper slits and their lower hooked returns.
  They now continue around the heel and join their respective side panels,
  rather than appearing only at the extreme edges of the rear view.
  The central webbing stays clear. The upper slots have dark backing layers.

  Rear Y/Z outlines are lofted toward the side panels with independent rake,
  vertical taper and outer-end flare. The medial upper profile narrows and
  slopes down into its side band; the lateral profile rises slightly.
  Redundant side endpoints were trimmed to reduce doubled junction contours,
  while the existing lower returns into the sole were preserved.
  These rear-to-side transitions are inferred because neither photograph
  supplies a calibrated rear-quarter view.

  The early heel stations now form fuller shoulders and a less pinched rear
  termination. The toe has a broader crown, a blunter rounded end and an
  asymmetric reinforcement boundary. Its rubber lip follows the forward
  toe-cap envelope instead of remaining at a fixed, partly buried position.
  More loft sections are concentrated at the tight toe and heel radii.
  Sole controls, fabric-lace routing and cougar-outline controls were retained.

  Thin panel skins now use directly linked outer/inner section lofts with
  annular end faces. This preserves their boundary surfaces without the slow
  subtraction of lofts sharing coincident end planes.

Fabric-lace follow-up
  The previous rounded-rectangle sections still read as thin paper strips.
  Flat-oval sections now provide a fuller crown and continuously rounded
  edges, while remaining distinctly wider than thick. Gentle span bow,
  gradual twist, local compression and rounded crossover arches replace
  the straight, sharp-folded ribbon appearance. Eyelet positions and the
  crossed routing are retained.
  This pass changes the fabric's overall form, not its weave texture.
  Fine fibers, tracer yarns and weave shading remain rendering-material work;
  the native preview continues to use a plain placeholder color.

Projection
  The original orthographic comparison exaggerated some front/heel
  discrepancies. Perspective is now used for the photographic comparison
  views, while the principal CAD inspection directions remain orthographic.
  Neither reference image is stretched to force agreement.

HOW TO INTERPRET ACCURACY

The visible contours were inspected against both supplied images, including
photo-space sketch overlays and corresponding CAD views. Image-space tracing
does not make the resulting three-dimensional reconstruction exact.

Remaining visible mismatches, beyond the deliberately omitted textures:
  - Rear returns and their side junctions remain more angular and heavier
    than the photographed trim, especially in the rear-quarter close-ups.
  - The toe crown and heel shoulders are fuller, but their exact compound
    curvature and the toe-cap edge are still approximations.
  - Lace placement is more regular than the photographed slack and crossings.
The refinements are not claimed to be exact matches.

The photographs provide no scale, calibrated lens, camera pose or physical
last. Their panel curves are mapped onto an inferred last using approximate
registration. Surface curvature, section depths, exact layer thicknesses,
the collar lining and hidden attachment details therefore remain estimates.
The underside tread layout is still inferred because no outsole photograph
was supplied. No numerical accuracy percentage or manufacturing accuracy is
claimed, and this pass should not be read as a pixel-perfect certification.

Placeholder colors are intentional. Knit structure, tracer yarns, color
gradients and printed markings other than the retained cougar graphic belong
to the visualization/material pass, not to geometric contour matching.
